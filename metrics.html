What to measure when an AI assistant talks to investors. Modules 1 to 5 followed a research assistant.     Measuring the P0s

    How to prove a zero

    Many P0 targets are "0". But finding no breaches in a sample doesn't mean there are none. Two questions decide whether a "0" on the dashboard means anything: how much did you check, and how good are the checks?

    Zero in a sample isn't zero overall

    If people review n replies and find no breaches, the most you can say with 95% confidence is that the true rate is below about 3 ÷ n. Statisticians call this the rule of three.

    –true breach rate could be as high as
    –breaches a month at that rate
    –replies to review, with none found, to show the target

    The checks need checking

    Automated checks run on 100% of replies, so they see far more than any reviewer. But a check that misses 1 breach in 5 will still report "0" on a bad day. That's why detector recall is now a P0 in the compliance category: measure how many known breaches the checks catch, before launch and on every release.

    • Seed a test set with real breaches from testing and red-teaming, labelled by Compliance.
    • Run the checks on it with every prompt, model or check change. Recall below the bar blocks the release.
    • Feed it with every breach that human review finds and the checks missed.

    Don't judge every reply in the reply path

    Running an LLM judge on every reply before it's sent roughly doubles model calls and adds seconds of waiting. Layer the checks instead, from cheap and instant to slow and thorough.

    LayerRuns onWhenCost and delayGood at
    Rules and small classifiers100% of repliesBefore sending: can blockMilliseconds, almost free"Assured returns", missing disclosures, numbers vs the NAV feed, PII patterns
    LLM-as-judge100% of replies, or a large sampleJust after sending: flags within minutesOne extra model call per reply judged, using a small model where it's accurate enoughAdvice-boundary wording, subtle nudges, made-up facts
    Human reviewEvery flag, plus a random sampleDaily and weeklyReviewer timeConfirming breaches, measuring how much the other layers miss

    Responding

    When a P0 fires: severity and runbook

    An alert is only useful if everyone knows what happens next. Agree severities and a runbook with Compliance, Engineering and Service before launch.

    SeverityLooks likeRespond withinFirst actions
    Sev 1A P0 breach that is ongoing or reached many investors: repeated assured-return claims, another investor's data shown, the approval step bypassed15 minutes, any timeSwitch off the bot or the affected intent, page Compliance and the data protection officer, preserve traces
    Sev 2A single confirmed P0 breach, contained: one advice-boundary reply, one stale NAVSame business dayFix or roll back the prompt, contact the investor if needed, search for similar conversations
    Sev 3P1 health slipping: rising escalations, latency or cost over targetNext weekly reviewFind the cause in traces, add test cases, plan the fix
    1. Detect. The alert fires and pages the named owner, not a shared inbox.
    2. Contain. Use the kill switch, turn off the affected intent, or move the prompt label back. Speed beats diagnosis here.
    3. Preserve. Export the affected traces and note the live model and prompt versions before anything changes.
    4. Assess. Filter traces to find every affected conversation and investor. Count them.
    5. Notify. Compliance and the data protection officer decide who must be told, and when, under your licences and laws such as the DPDP Act.
    6. Fix and test. Add every bad conversation to the eval set and the seeded compliance set. Re-run before releasing the fix.
    7. Review. Hold a blameless review within a week. Update checks, thresholds and this runbook.

    Keep this

    P0 launch checklist and review rhythm

    Every P0 metric in one list. Before go-live, each one should be measured, alerting, and owned by a named person. Tick them off as you confirm them with your teams. Ticks are saved in this browser only.

    P0 checklist

      Review rhythm

      Real timeAlerts on any P0 breach: prohibited claims, advice-boundary breaches, PII exposure, unauthorised access, successful injections, outages, cost runaway.
      WeeklyP1 review: Compliance samples transcripts, eval results for every change, escalation quality, top failure themes, latency and cost.
      MonthlyCompliance scorecard, complaint trends, fairness slices, cost per resolution, P2 trends.
      QuarterlyBoard and audit pack: model inventory, incidents, audit-trail completeness, regulatory changes and their impact.

      Questions to ask before launch

      • Which P0 metrics are automated today, and which depend on manual sampling?
      • Who gets paged for each P0 alert, and what's the response time?
      • Can we reproduce any conversation from six months ago, exactly?
      • Who signs off a prompt or model change, and where is that recorded?
      • How do we know our automated compliance checks aren't missing things? What's their recall on the seeded set?
      • How many replies does Compliance review each week, and what does a clean sample actually prove?
      • Who can switch the bot off, and has anyone practised it?
